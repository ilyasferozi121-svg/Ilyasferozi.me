01

Foreign Policy and Diplomacy

This theme examines how small and middle powers formulate foreign policy priorities, navigate asymmetric alliances, and deploy bilateral and multilateral diplomacy to advance national interests. Particular attention is given to South Asia and the broader Middle East, focusing on how domestic institutional dynamics and shifting systemic pressures shape foreign policy decision-making and regional cooperation.

Key Research Questions

  • How do developing and middle powers balance competing great-power alignments without forfeiting foreign policy autonomy?
  • To what extent do domestic political institutions and bureaucratic actors shape strategic shifts in bilateral diplomacy across South Asia?
  • Under what conditions can economic and multilateral diplomacy foster durable regional cooperation amid protracted geopolitical rivalries?

Concepts & Literature: Foreign Policy Analysis (FPA), Neoclassical Realism, Strategic Hedging, Small and Middle Power Diplomacy, Regional Security Complex Theory (RSCT), Economic Statecraft.

02

Regional and Indian Ocean Security

This area explores intensifying strategic competition, naval modernization, and evolving governance architectures across the Indian Ocean Region (IOR). It investigates how major and littoral states manage maritime security challenges, protect critical sea lines of communication (SLOCs), and negotiate overlapping connectivity initiatives in an increasingly contested maritime order.

Key Research Questions

  • How is intensifying US–China–India strategic competition reshaping maritime security alignments and port infrastructure politics in the northern Indian Ocean?
  • What mechanisms of maritime domain awareness and multilateral governance are most effective for addressing non-traditional threats such as piracy, trafficking, and marine degradation?
  • How do littoral states in the Arabian Sea balance commercial port development with broader regional security imperatives?

Concepts & Literature: Maritime Security, Sea Lines of Communication (SLOCs), Geoeconomics and Connectivity Politics, Strategic Hedging in Littoral States, Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA), Indo-Pacific Regional Order.

03

Climate and Environmental Security

This theme investigates the pathways through which climate change, ecological degradation, and extreme weather events act as threat multipliers for national and human security. Focusing on climate-vulnerable regions in South Asia, it examines how environmental stress interacts with resource scarcity, cross-border water governance, and state adaptation capacity.

Key Research Questions

  • How do recurring climate shocks—such as floods, droughts, and coastal erosion—compound existing socioeconomic vulnerabilities and strain state governance capacity?
  • In what ways do transboundary water stress and environmental degradation affect bilateral relations and cooperative frameworks in South Asia?
  • How effectively do national climate adaptation policies translate international climate commitments into localized human security outcomes?

Concepts & Literature: Environmental Security, Securitization Theory, Human Security, Climate–Conflict Nexus, Transboundary Water Governance, Climate Adaptation and Resilience Policy.

04

Pakistan's Mineral Diplomacy

This research area analyzes the intersection of critical mineral wealth, foreign direct investment, and geopolitical competition in Pakistan's external relations. It examines how Pakistan leverages strategic mineral reserves—such as copper, gold, and rare earth elements—to attract international partnerships while navigating intensifying US–China competition over global critical mineral supply chains.

Key Research Questions

  • How does Pakistan structure its mineral diplomacy to attract foreign capital and technical expertise while safeguarding long-term national economic sovereignty?
  • To what extent has global competition over critical mineral supply chains reshaped US, Chinese, and Gulf engagement with Pakistan's extractive sector?
  • What regulatory, institutional, and geopolitical risks influence the sustainability of foreign investment in large-scale mining projects?

Concepts & Literature: International Political Economy (IPE), Critical Minerals and Supply Chain Geopolitics, Resource Nationalism, Extractive Governance, Geoeconomics, Asymmetric Interdependence.

05

Security and Governance in Balochistan

This theme examines the interconnected dynamics of political stability, subnational governance, and regional security in Balochistan. Positioned at the crossroads of South Asia, Central Asia, and the Arabian Sea, the province offers a critical lens for studying how center–periphery relations, infrastructure development, and cross-border dynamics influence human security and long-term stability.

Key Research Questions

  • How do governance deficits, center–periphery fiscal arrangements, and local institutional capacity affect long-term political stability in Balochistan?
  • In what ways do transboundary security dynamics along the Pakistan–Iran and Pakistan–Afghanistan borders shape provincial security governance?
  • How can mega-infrastructure and resource-extraction initiatives be aligned with inclusive socioeconomic development to strengthen human security?

Concepts & Literature: Subnational Governance and Center–Periphery Relations, Human Security and Development Nexus, Borderland Studies, Transboundary Security, State Capacity and Institutional Legitimacy.
